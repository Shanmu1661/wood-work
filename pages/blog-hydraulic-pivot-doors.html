Door Engineering

Concealed Hydraulic Pivots for 300 lb Hardwood Doors

By Marcus Vance (Master Joiner) • Published Oct 18, 2026 • 6 min read

Grand entrance doors measuring 3.0 meters tall by 1.6 meters wide create an awe-inspiring transition between exterior and interior. When crafted from solid Burma Teak or European White Oak, such door slabs readily weigh between 140kg and 220kg (300 to 500 lbs). Hanging a slab of this magnitude on standard edge butt hinges is a mechanical recipe for catastrophic sagging and frame twisting.

1. Why Butt Hinges Fail on Oversized Doors

Traditional side hinges transfer 100% of the door’s rotational cantilever weight directly into the lateral door frame jamb. As door width increases beyond 1000mm, the lateral leverage multiplies exponentially. Over time, hinge screws pull from the timber studs, bottom edges scrape hardwood flooring, and perimeter latch bolts fail to align.

2. The Floor-Embedded Hydraulic Advantage

By embedding the hydraulic pivot mechanism directly into the bottom of the door slab and anchoring the base plate 8mm into the concrete floor, gravity becomes the door's ally rather than its enemy. Vertical compressive load transfers cleanly downward, while the top pivot functions purely as an alignment guide experiencing almost zero lateral strain. Even a 200kg door can be glided open with the light push of a single finger.

3. Dual Automatic Drop Seals & Magnetic Perimeter Gaskets

Because pivot doors swing past both the inside and outside floor planes, traditional rebate stops cannot be used along the pivot stile. We solve acoustic and weather insulation by routing twin automatic drop-down neoprene sweep seals into the bottom rail. When the door swings closed, an internal mechanical trigger deploys the seals against the floor threshold, blocking drafts, driving rain, and exterior street noise.

Floor Foundation Pre-Requisite

Never install a 300 lb pivot base plate into floating timber flooring or hollow screed. The 8mm base plate anchor pins must penetrate directly into the structural reinforced concrete slab with two-part anchoring epoxy to resist dynamic opening torques over decades.
